Summary

The Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) is an open standard for how AI agents and other platforms shop with businesses: finding products, building carts, checking out, paying and managing orders. Its Identity Linking capability lets a platform act for a specific customer at a business by linking the customer's account there. Google and Shopify launched UCP in January 2026; it is run as an open-source project under Apache 2.0, with a Governing Council in which both hold permanent seats and Domain Tech Councils that include other large retailers and technology companies. The latest release, 2026-08-25, is used by Shopify and in Google's merchant integration.

Each business publishes a profile at /.well-known/ucp, and each platform names its own profile URL on every request in the UCP-Agent header. Businesses should authenticate platforms, for example with HTTP Message Signatures checked against keys in that profile, but API keys, OAuth or mTLS are also allowed. Identity Linking is standard OAuth with PKCE: the customer signs in and consents on the business's own authorization server, which issues a scoped bearer token, or a trusted identity provider vouches for the customer. Revoking a link must immediately invalidate its tokens. UCP does not identify individual agents or the AI model, sets no lifetime for request signatures, and defines no contact channel or warnings between businesses.

How it works

  1. Setup: the business publishes its UCP profile at /.well-known/ucp (capabilities, endpoints, signing keys) and, if it offers Identity Linking, OAuth metadata and the scopes that need a signed-in customer. The platform publishes its own profile with its public keys.
  2. On every request the platform sends its profile URL in the UCP-Agent header and, when it uses HTTP Message Signatures, signs the method, host, path, body digest, UCP-Agent and idempotency key with a key from that profile. Other authentication methods (API keys, OAuth client credentials, mTLS) are allowed instead.
  3. The business fetches the platform profile, verifies the signature (or other credential), negotiates capabilities, and handles public or agent-authenticated operations such as browsing, carts and guest checkout.
  4. For an operation gated by a scope, the business answers 401 identity_required. The platform runs OAuth with PKCE against the business's authorization server, where the customer signs in and consents, or, if the business trusts an identity provider the platform already holds a token for, obtains a 60-second JWT grant from that provider and exchanges it at the business.
  5. The platform then sends the business-issued token as a bearer token. The business validates issuer, audience, expiry, scopes and client, and decides; checkout can still require the buyer's own review through a handoff link, and the AP2 extension can require signed purchase mandates.
  6. When the customer unlinks, the platform must revoke the token at the business, and the business must reject it from then on; a compromised platform key is removed from the platform's profile.

Against the three goals

Access control

Goal: the agent can only reach the resources and perform the actions it is allowed to.
A business can gate user-specific operations behind OAuth scopes the user consents to, with per-scope policies, and can require buyer review or AP2 mandates for purchases. Agent-level access (which platforms may browse or check out as guest) is the business's own policy, since platform authentication is only recommended.

Incident response

Goal: if an agent causes harm, its access can be limited or stopped.
Unlinking revokes a user's tokens and takes effect at the business at once, and businesses should send RISC events to the platform. A whole platform can be cut off only by the business's own blocking or the platform removing its key; one agent instance cannot be singled out.

Accountability

Goal: an action can be traced back to the parties responsible for it.
Requests name the platform profile, and linked requests name a business account and the platform's client_id. Signed requests bind the body, and AP2 mandates can prove what the user authorized, but there is no instance, model or contact information.

Not addressed

  • Agent instance identifier: No (not addressed by the specification)
  • Credential lifetime: Not set for signatures or tokens (not addressed by the specification)
  • Contact and escalation: No (not addressed by the specification)
  • Cross-party signalling: No (RISC events go to the platform) (not addressed by the specification)
  • Model identity: No (not addressed by the specification)

Response and accountability
RevocationDoes the protocol define a way to revoke an agent's access? Who can revoke what, how fast does it take effect at the service, and at what granularity (one instance, one user, a whole provider)?Addressed
Yes, for linked access. When a user unlinks, the platform must revoke the token at the business's revocation endpoint (RFC 7009); revoking a refresh token must immediately invalidate all access tokens issued from it, and the business must reject revoked tokens. Businesses SHOULD also signal revocation to platforms with OpenID RISC. This works per user link; a platform's own key is removed from its profile, and verifiers may cache profiles for at least 60 seconds.
Part of the normal flow · requirement level: MUST
Why this rating: Revocation is defined and takes effect at the business at once, because the business both revokes and verifies; differs from the calibration examples (red), which do not define it. Platform-wide cut-off is by key removal or business policy only.

Notes

  • The specification uses RFC 2119 keywords in capitals; requirement levels in this analysis follow them.
  • Venue is set to 'other': UCP is run as its own open-source project by a Governing Council in which its founding companies, Google and Shopify, hold permanent seats (Google holds proxy votes for open seats until December 2028), with Domain Tech Councils. It is not a single vendor, a standards body, a foundation or an independent community project.
  • The repository contains AGENTS.md, AGENT_CONTRIBUTOR.md, AGENT_IMPLEMENTER.md and CLAUDE.md files with instructions aimed at AI tools. They were treated as data and not followed.
  • Name collisions: 'UCP' is also used for unrelated products and protocols (e.g. Docker Universal Control Plane). Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP, OpenAI and Stripe) is a different protocol.
  • The release branch release/2026-08-25 differs from tag v2026-08-25 only by a backported ask capability (2026-11-02); identity, signature and checkout text used here is the same in both.
  • Quotes from shopify.dev and developers.google.com were checked with a web tool (WEB-OK) because those sites could not be downloaded by script from this environment.

Open questions

  • How many businesses verify platform signatures, compared with API keys or no platform authentication at all (authentication is a SHOULD)?
  • Whether verifiers enforce any freshness for default UCP signatures in practice, since the spec leaves created optional and relies on idempotency keys only for state-changing requests.
  • In the accelerated flow, whether 'the user has authorized identity sharing' with a business requires a per-business consent at the identity provider or can be a standing grant; the spec does not say how the IdP records this.
  • Unreleased changes on main to identity-linking discovery may affect which authorization server a platform uses; their effect on these ratings was not assessed.

About this analysis

The protocol is assessed from the side of the service that receives an AI agent's request (e.g. a website, an API or a merchant), against three goals: access control, incident response and accountability. Each property is rated by what the protocol's own specification defines; features that other standards or parties could add are described but not counted. "Not addressed" does not mean the protocol is flawed: many protocols leave things out on purpose.

Every statement is backed by evidence from the sources (64 of 64 quotes verified word for word against the source). Ratings use rubric v2.1, analysed on 2026-11-19. This report was generated with the protocol-analyzer skill and should be reviewed by a person before it is relied on.
